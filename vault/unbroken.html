Unbroken

Unbroken is an action war movie released in 2014 and directed by Angelina Jolie. The film grossed approximately $163 million at the global box office. It is a semi-biographical epic based on Laura Hillenbrand’s 2010 non-fiction book and explores the true-life experiences of Louis "Louie" Zamperini during World War II. Jolie’s direction, alongside the film’s historical context, sets the stage for a cinematic experience that is a quintessential example of the resilience of the human spirit and the brutal realities of war-time captivity.

The story begins with a series of flashbacks to Louie’s youth as a rebellious Italian-American child who finds his path through track and field, eventually competing in the 1936 Berlin Olympics. We then jump into his service as a bombardier in the Army Air Forces during the Pacific theater of WWII. During a search-and-rescue mission, his plane suffers mechanical failure and crashes into the ocean. The plot then follows Louie’s miraculous survival alongside two other crewmates, Phil and Mac, as they drift for 47 days in a small life raft, surviving shark attacks and starvation before being captured by the Japanese Navy. The remainder of the film focuses on Louie’s time in various POW camps, specifically his psychological and physical battle against the sadistic guard Mutsuhiro "The Bird" Watanabe.

The creative elements of the film distinguish it as a high-quality production, particularly in its visual storytelling. Jack O'Connell delivers a visceral, physically demanding performance that relies on the portrayal of endurance rather than dialogue. Legendary cinematographer Roger Deakins used high-contrast lighting and wide shots to capture the isolation of the Pacific Ocean and the claustrophobic, dusty atmosphere of the labor camps. The mood is heavy and persistent, supported by a sweeping score by Alexandre Desplat and sound effects that make the mechanical failures and camp life feel personal and immediate. Symbolic imagery, such as Louie being forced to hold a heavy wooden beam over his head, adds a layer of spiritual defiance to the film’s grim feel.

In conclusion, Angelina Jolie was very successful in her purpose of creating an immersive, sensory-driven war drama. By focusing on the direct physical toll of Louie’s journey, the environment of the POW camps feels more like a character than a setting. Evidence of this success is found in the raft sequences, where the lack of resources makes the environment feel like a constant threat. Between the straightforward plot and excellent acting, Unbroken is an iconic example of a modern war film. However, it does have some downsides, such as focusing so heavily on the repetitive nature of the camp's brutality that it leaves out much of Louie’s post-war journey toward forgiveness. Nevertheless, it is a very good example of an enjoyable and impactful film.

Infinite

Infinite is a Science Fiction action movie that was adapted from “The Reincarnationist Papers,” a novel written by D. Eric Maikranz. It was released in 2021, and due to COVID-19 was only shown in theatres for a short time, amassing only 1.3 million in its limited showings. It is generally considered to be a mediocre at best film, with little plot, unoriginal themes, and lousy acting. At the same time, the film has collected a cult-like following of people, who admire it for its beautiful shots, high intensity, and open universe.

The story begins with a flashback sequence that involves a car chase, including a character called Treadway, as he runs from an unknown threat. He is bleeding, and on a call to another character, says that if he doesn't make it, to “Look Inside”. We cut to a man by the name of Evan McCauley (played by Mark Wahlberg). Evan was diagnosed with schizophrenia at the age of 14. He has skills that he uses that feel more like a memory, and can’t remember how he got them. Evan ends up arrested and held captive until a new character appears who we later learn is a man called Bathurst, who threatens Evan until he starts to have flashbacks. A car bursts through the wall and rescues Evan. The lady inside tells him that there is a group of about 500 souls on earth who can remember all of their past lives, called Infinites. They are split between two factions. They believe Evan is the reincarnation of Treadway (from the opening flashback). Treadway was the only one who knew where “The Egg” was hidden, a weapon capable of wiping out all life on earth. Bathurst finds the egg, and as the leader of the Nihilists (The opposing faction), takes The Egg and preps it to be deployed. The rest of the plot follows Evan and his battle against Bathurst. Evan ends up defeating Bathurst for good, dying in the process, and the film ends, closing with scenes from the character's future reincarnation cycle.

There are many creative elements that were used in this film. Some were very bland and dry, but some made the film come to life. The best elements in the film were subjectively the lighting and the use of wind and shimmering special effects help the small amount of magic in the film come to life. The fast cuts in the combat scenes help it to move and flow, and help it to be fast paced. The use of flashbacks creates a sense of depth in the world, drastically assisting the flat feeling plot. For example, the opening flashback depicting Evan's past life as Treadway creates a sense that there is a lot more to his character than we actually know.

Overall, Infinite is an entertaining film. Its plot does feel flat, and we don’t get in depth with the characters. However, this leaves room for imaginative fans, and its fast paced fights (especially the final battle with Bathurst) and great visuals are enough to keep the average viewer engaged for its rough hour-and-a-half view time. It's an example of a classic Sci-Fi movie in the likeness of The Matrix, and overall an okay film.

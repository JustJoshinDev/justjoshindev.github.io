Free Solo

Free Solo is an action/adventure documentary film that was released in 2018 with a box office of $29.2 million worldwide. It set a record for the highest earning average per-venue for a documentary ever. Free Solo is an awe inspiring story of a real life adventure that is loved for its beautiful cinematography, especially in IMAX. It features a plethora of personal struggles and pieces of everyday life that the public can relate to, and is a quintessential example of what a documentary can be, combining a narrative, personal struggle, and education, all shown in an hour and 40 minutes.

The documentary follows rock climber Alex Hammond, popular for his free climbing skills, as he prepares and climbs El Capitan in Yosemite National Park with no ropes or assistance from other climbers. We learn a little about his past with climbing, and jump right into preparations. They climb with ropes, scouting out the route up the mountain and testing, trying, and practicing different techniques and moves they have to use. After prep, Alex starts to climb… and backs out a little ways up. A little more prep later, and when he doesn't feel like he's being watched, Alex scales El Capitan in less than 4 hours, free soloing the entire thing. The movie also tracks Alex’s romantic life with his girlfriend, Sanni. At the beginning of the movie, Alex says he doesn't really want to be in a relationship. Though there is conflict throughout (specifically regarding Alex’s life threatening mission to free solo El Capitan), by the time Alex completes his climb, they own a house together. Today, in the present day Alex and Sanni are married with two kids.

Free Solo makes use of a handful of different techniques. It uses plenty of wide shots of the mountain, as well as close ups of Alex to show details as he climbs. There is also a montage included when talking about the risk of free soloing, showcasing climbers that have passed from falling. It was filmed for viewing in IMAX for an immersive experience, and they shot at all times of day. They used massive zoom lenses for some shots in order to stay out of Alex’s way and still get detailed shots, even from the ground. They filmed in chronological order, switching between climbing sessions and interviews as the journey went on.

Overall, Free Solo is a solid documentary. It shows off nature in all of its beauty, a relationship that is messy yet heartwarming, and it puts on a display of courage, hard work, and audacity on Alex’s part. However, it does have some downsides. For example, It would have been nice to have seen more climbing and had a focus on the struggles with the journey, and to have focused much less on the emotional and relational struggles between Alex and Sanni. It felt like the documentary was more about their relationship than the climb at one point in time. Free Solo is a documentary that beautifully captures nature and a journey, and is a very good example of an enjoyable and relatable film.
